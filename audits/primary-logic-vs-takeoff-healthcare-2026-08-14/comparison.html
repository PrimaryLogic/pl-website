Competitive sales brief · 14 Aug 2026

Takeoff makes the buyer want the outcome. Primary Logic makes the buyer trust the mechanism.

The opportunity is to keep Primary Logic’s narrower, safer wedge while borrowing Takeoff’s commercial confidence, economic framing, and visual immediacy.

The verdict

Takeoff wins desire. Primary Logic wins diligence.

Takeoff turns patient leakage into lost revenue, shows a vivid “coordination team as software,” and states an economic result. Primary Logic explains the second-pass operating model with unusually strong boundaries—but delays the payoff and reads like a pilot memo before it reads like a growth offer.

Best strategic position

“The performance layer for referral revenue your current process leaves behind.”

Narrower than Takeoff. More valuable than “administrative follow-through.”

Demand-side forces

The sale happens when push and pull outweigh anxiety and habit. Takeoff amplifies the first two; Primary Logic is strongest on the last two.

Push

Why change now?

Takeoff: every dropped patient is revenue already paid for; manual outreach takes days.

Primary Logic: the next action loses its owner, but the commercial consequence is understated.

Fix: quantify the recoverable cohort and cost of another month of leakage.

Pull

What better future?

Takeoff: a tireless coordination team that remembers every interaction and converts more patients.

Primary Logic: one owner follows every approved next action to verified evidence.

Fix: translate discipline into filled schedules and recovered contribution margin.

Anxiety

What could go wrong?

Primary Logic: answers integration, PHI, clinical boundaries, attribution, disputes, and payment risk.

Takeoff: offers HIPAA and escalation assurances but asserts more than it explains.

Preserve this advantage, but move most detail below an economically stronger first screen.

Habit

Why keep the status quo?

The real incumbents are staff retry routines, BPOs, generic automation, paid acquisition, and accepting leakage as normal.

Primary Logic: minimizes disruption. Takeoff: attacks BPO and paid-media economics directly.

Name both truths: “keep your first pass; stop paying twice for its misses.”

Guidance

Do not copy Takeoff’s broad category claim or unsupported proof posture. Borrow the architecture that makes economic value legible, then make Primary Logic’s evidence standard the differentiator.

1

Lead with recovered revenue, not the pilot apparatus

Put the economic contract in the hero: the cohort already exists, the first pass already spent the labor, and Primary Logic is paid only when the customer’s record shows a kept first visit.

2

Show the handoff in one diagram

Compress the model into: your first pass → unresolved tail → Primary Logic second pass → kept visit in your EHR → outcome fee. This is the visual Takeoff has and Primary Logic currently makes visitors assemble.

3

Turn safety from the story into the proof beneath the story

Keep BAA, consent, human judgment, authoritative-system, and $0 non-outcome language. Reduce its share of the first two screens so the page first earns desire, then resolves anxiety.

4

Replace product proof you do not have with buyer-specific evidence

Until pilots produce named results, use a cohort-value calculator, a sample reconciliation report, a concrete 30-day pilot scorecard, and an explicit proof plan. Label modeled inputs and outputs.

5

Make the CTA perform the buyer’s first job

“Estimate recoverable referral revenue” or “Size a second-pass cohort” creates value before a sales call and feels more concrete than “Discuss a pilot.”
