Competitive value architecture · 13 August 2026

Primary Logic is missing proof, not another savings claim.

Sierra, Takeoff, and Assort make different choices, but all avoid asking a buyer to believe a synthetic ROI model before establishing the billable unit, the incumbent cost, and credible operating evidence.

Overview

The critical misses

Remove now

“Only one transfers ownership” is not defensible.

Takeoff explicitly sells jobs completed end to end across channels. Assort says it orchestrates the full patient journey. Sierra sells completed outcomes rather than software usage. Primary Logic may own a more specific referral contract, but ownership in the abstract is no longer distinctive.

Commercial issue

The current example gives Primary Logic nearly all the value.

The illustrative case creates $3.15M of gross annual value, then charges $2.40M in outcome fees plus $250K implementation. The customer keeps $500K: a 1.19× benefit-cost ratio and 18.9% ROI before execution risk.

What the market teaches

Sell the unit before the total invoice.

Sierra defines resolved work. Takeoff defines revenue-aligned work. Assort defines operating outcomes through customer evidence. Primary Logic currently jumps from a $100 unit to a $500K monthly bill without first proving the unit's conservative value.

What buyers trust

Proof stacks, not one grand ROI number.

Assort layers revenue, labor capacity, hours saved, hold time, and satisfaction. Sierra layers conversion, service quality, and hiring avoided. The evidence forms a causal chain; the financial result is the conclusion, not the opening assumption.

The current illustrative case, restated honestly

$1.95M incremental contribution
+ $1.20M operating cost removed or avoided
− $2.40M outcome fees
− $0.25M implementation
= $0.50M customer net value

Primary Logic plus implementation captures 84% of the modeled gross value. That is too little customer margin for uncertain lift, change management, integration risk, provider-capacity constraints, or a failure to remove actual cost.

Its page claims Takeoff is a fixed cost that gets cheaper at scale, while Takeoff's June 2026 company post says agents are priced on outcomes and spend grows with completed work. The page also displays “$0 upfront” where an actual Takeoff price should be. Borrow the denominator and causal comparison—not the unsupported numbers or inconsistency.

Guidance

Rebuild the value story in this order

01

Replace the ownership claim with a precise contract.

Say what becomes Primary Logic's responsibility: every permitted administrative next step in an assigned referral lane until an EHR-verified kept first appointment or another agreed terminal state. Then show the residual customer work, escalation boundary, write-back, and invoice event.

02

Lead with three measurable value pools.

Capacity: hiring and backfills avoided, referrals per FTE, hours returned, after-hours coverage. Growth: incremental kept appointments that available providers can actually serve. Reliability: time to first action, percent reaching a known terminal state, queue age, duplicate work, and leakage.

03

Show value per billed outcome before annual spend.

For the current example, each billed kept appointment carries $81.25 of incremental contribution allocated across all 24,000 kept outcomes plus $50 of operating cost removed: $131.25 gross value per billed outcome. A $100 fee leaves $31.25 before implementation and only $20.83 after it. The thin margin is visible immediately.

04

Set a customer-value floor, not merely a list price.

Do not deploy at $100 because that is the nominal price. Reprice or disqualify unless conservative, finance-approved value clears total fees and implementation by a meaningful risk margin. A 1.19× modeled benefit-cost ratio is not enough for a new operating model.

05

Use observed evidence before a public savings promise.

Until Primary Logic has customer proof, present a buyer-controlled model as a qualification tool, not a headline claim. Once deployments exist, replace assumptions with case-study evidence: baseline, intervention, outcome, time window, and what cost was actually removed, avoided, or redeployed.

Recommended website sequence

  1. The operating contract: what Primary Logic owns, what it does not, and when the workflow closes.
  2. The real alternatives: staff/BPO, point automation, patient-access agents, and Primary Logic—compared by scope, residual work, evidence, and pay trigger.
  3. The unit economics: conservative value per verified kept outcome versus fee per outcome.
  4. The proof stack: capacity, conversion, reliability, and financial results from actual deployments.
  5. The buyer model: editable assumptions, with no vendor forecast presented as fact.

Confidence and gaps

The positioning and case-study claims above come from official vendor-controlled sources and are current as of 13 August 2026.
